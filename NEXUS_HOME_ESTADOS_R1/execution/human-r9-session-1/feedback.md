# First task in the Home usability session

The participant reported that they found the collaborator and understood how
to inspect the work. They identified four sources of friction.

- The session identifier was visible in work inspection. The expandable
  session details showed several identifiers without a reason to use them.
  A terminal command would give that information a practical purpose.
- “Work request” did not explain what the collaborator was working on.
- The connection disclosure and its evidence timestamp occupied space
  without giving the participant an action.
- The result used a raw UTC timestamp instead of a readable local time.

The observer recorded a click on View work, exploration of the work and
result panels, and expansion of both disclosures. The first recorded click
came after the original question expired. The participant’s feedback
qualifies the observed usability of inspection. Live work state and question
response are still open evaluation tasks. No participant time measurement is
used as a gate. Only the participant’s report establishes their experience.

This was the installed R9 candidate recorded in `session.json`, with an inert
peer and zero provider calls. Browser requests were GET only. The fixture and
browser stopped normally and the loopback listener was absent afterward.
One canonical session close was confirmed and two remained unconfirmed.
The public files were checked against the temporary operator cookie value
before archiving. This session leaves the prior novice-user gate open.
