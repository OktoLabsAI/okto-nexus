# M7r: real unlimited concurrency and expired-grant refusal

Date: 2026-10-18. A disposable Nexus 0.3.1 installed from its wheel ran on `127.0.0.1:8204` with Connector 0.0.10 and Core 0.0.12. The global Nexus on port 8202 remained stopped. The fixture was a copy of the prior UI-test home; no production agent or database was changed.

The first real-UI burst configured `m7-codex` with `max_parallel=0`, zero warm instances and queue capacity three. Three Meta-harness tabs sent distinct calls together. All remained `ADMITTED` without a native session while the pool repeatedly attempted `runtime.open` (72 operations observed). The copied fixture's grant had expired. Each open had terminal `PERMISSION_DENIED`, `possible_effect=false` and no Core receipt, but its refusal only released the logical slot; the waiting calls were not failed, so demand created another opening. This is a permanent pre-Core refusal, not a slow harness startup.

The Nexus now propagates that exact permanent no-effect authorization refusal to pending calls for the same agent, executor and configuration generation. The callers receive `FAILED/PERMISSION_DENIED`; clearing demand stops repeated openings. The targeted source refusal suite passed 12 tests. Four selected installed-wheel regression cases passed with pytest source imports disabled. After installing the corrected wheel into the disposable environment and restarting only that instance, all three stranded calls became `FAILED` with `execution_started=false` and `possible_effect=false`; their error messages were visible in the Meta-harness, and no pool slots remained.

The grant was then renewed through the Nexus UI with unlimited duration and actions; the connection test succeeded. Three tabs sent `M7R2_CODEX_01/02/03_OK` requests at 15:29:46–47 UTC. Three independent Codex native processes and three pool slots were observed `RUNNING` together. Call starts were 15:30:27.932, 15:30:28.978 and 15:30:30.203 UTC; the first final reply arrived at 15:30:36.586 UTC, establishing at least 6.38 seconds with all three calls running concurrently. All three calls ended `SUCCEEDED`, the Meta-harness displayed the three exact distinct replies with 1/1 ACK, and the slots cleared. Minimum sampled free RAM during this run exceeded 10 GiB, above the fixed 4 GiB abort floor.

This closes the previously open local M7 unlimited-mode criterion above two real concurrent instances. Together with M7o's fixed installed 54/54 distribution, the finite pool, queue, rejection, deadline, warm/cold and local Connector/embedded UI cases, M7 and local UI-B acceptance are complete. This is not physical remote-host qualification; the user's selected Connector-local topology remains the boundary of this gate.

The disposable Nexus process tree and Playwright browser were stopped. Neither port 8202 nor 8204 had a listener afterward; free RAM was 9.88 GiB.
